Rules

The numbers apply to the default settings. Stock size, hand size and jokers can be changed in the app.

Goal

Be the first to empty your own stock.

Cards

The game uses 144 number cards (twelve each of the numbers 1 to 12) and 18 jokers. Each player gets a face-down stock of 30 cards; only the top card is visible. You also hold 5 hand cards.

Build piles

Four build piles lie in the middle. Each starts with a 1 and grows by exactly one number: 1, 2, 3 up to 12. A completed pile (up to the 12) is cleared away and reshuffled later. A joker stands in for any number.

Your turn

At the start of your turn your hand is refilled to 5 cards. Then you may play as many cards as you like: from your hand, the top card of your stock, or the top card of one of your discards. If you play all your hand cards, you draw a new hand at once. Your turn ends when you put a hand card on one of your discards.

Discards

Every player has four discards of their own. Cards are stacked and only the top card can be played. Sort wisely: small numbers on top and larger ones below let you play them in the right order later. Everybody can see all discards.

Time per turn

You have 60 seconds per turn. When time runs out, your highest hand card is put on your smallest discard. The clock only runs while your hand cards are visible.

End and deadlocked games

Whoever plays the last card of their stock wins at once. If no stock card is played for 60 turns per player, the game ends: the player with the smallest stock wins; a tie has no winner.

Controls

Tap a card and then its target, or drag the card there. Possible targets glow green. If you have no hand cards and nothing is left to draw, end your turn with “End turn”.

Several players on one device

After every turn the hand is hidden. Pass the device on; the next player taps the screen and sees their cards. Stocks and discards are always visible.

Computer opponents

Beginner plays at random and often misses moves. Medium plays its stock first and plans one step ahead. Hard plans whole card sequences, saves jokers and does not help opponents. Expert plays without mistakes, blocks opponents with a small stock and uses jokers in the endgame. Computers never see other players' hands.

Pile Race is a game by Robert Peringer.
